Case study 01 — Undergraduate thesis

Hazard zones in seconds, not simulation queues

ALOHA dispersion modeling answers one scenario at a time. For n-Hexane tank overfill, I built ML surrogates that answer 1,261 of them — then shipped the result as a live web app with an interactive plume map.

Problem
Consequence modeling for overfill scenarios is run case-by-case in ALOHA, which is too slow for wide-range screening or rapid emergency-response decisions.
Approach
Generated 1,215 full-factorial scenarios across six parameters (Pasquill stability class, wind, temperature, spill rate, duration, liquid thickness), extended to 1,261 with targeted out-of-distribution additions, then trained MLR, Random Forest, and ANN surrogates to predict red/orange/yellow LEL zone radii.
Outcome
The ANN surrogate reaches R² 0.9975 with a red-zone MAE of 1.82 m — 12× lower than the MLR baseline — deployed as a Flask web app with an ALOHA-style plume map, batch processing, and KML export, plus a BI dashboard for scenario analytics.
  • 1,261 scenarios
  • ANN R² 0.9975
  • 3 LEL zones
  • Live web app

Case study 02 — First author · accepted, SUSTINERE (Scopus-indexed · SINTA 2)

Stress-testing Indonesia’s energy transition targets

Policy targets are usually debated in words. This study puts numbers on them: probabilistic feasibility of Indonesia’s renewable energy goals under uncertainty.

Problem
Indonesia’s renewable targets lacked a quantified, uncertainty-aware feasibility assessment grounded in historical energy data.
Approach
ARIMA forecasting of energy supply to 2060, multiple regression linking renewable and non-renewable energy to CO₂ emissions, and 10,000-iteration Monte Carlo simulation with sensitivity analysis.
Outcome
The 2025 renewable target shows a 65.21% probability of achievement; the 2060 APS 78% target shows 0.00% under current trends. Accepted for publication in SUSTINERE: Journal of Environment and Sustainability (Scopus-indexed, SINTA 2).
  • 10,000 MC iterations
  • 65.21% vs 0.00%
  • 2060 horizon

Case study 03 — Published · Motivection (national accredited journal)

Quantifying human error in the Boeing 737 MAX case

Human error dominates safety-critical failures but resists honest quantification. As project leader, I compared two Human Reliability Assessment methods on a single high-stakes case.

Problem
HEART and SLIM can yield different pictures of human error probability — which method should safety engineers trust, and when?
Approach
Applied both methods to a Boeing 737 MAX accident case study, computing human error probabilities and comparing assumptions, sensitivity, and interpretability.
Outcome
A published comparative framework for method selection in Human Reliability Assessment, peer-reviewed in a nationally accredited journal.
